# Answers

## Snippet 1

```text
HITL:deploy("Deploy to production?") AUTO
WHEN deploy.yes
  → EMIT deployment
```

**VALID.**

- The `HITL` line matches the required form `HITL:<name>("<message>") [AUTO]` (Syntax table: `HITL[:<name>]("<message>") [AUTO]`; and the `AUTO` section: "`AUTO` is written directly after the closing parenthesis of `HITL(...)`, on the same line."). It also matches the grammar production: `hitl = "HITL" , [":" , identifier] , "(" , string , ")" , ["AUTO"] , NL , [...]`.
- `deploy` is a valid `<name>` and, per the `HITL` section, "The response establishes the outcome `<name>.<answer>`, where `<answer>` is one of the answers tested by the `WHEN <name>.<answer>` constructs that follow." Here `WHEN deploy.yes` tests the answer `yes` for the decision `deploy`, which is exactly the pattern the spec describes.
- `deploy.yes` is a well-formed `condition`: `condition = identifier , "." , identifier ;` and the Conditional Flow section confirms `<condition>` has the form `<identifier>.<identifier>`.
- The `WHEN` block's `→ EMIT deployment` is a valid flow item (`EMIT` takes an identifier argument, matching `simple = ("REQUIRE"|"DELEGATE"|"VERIFY"|"TRANSITION"|"EMIT"), argument, NL`).
- No `FALLBACK` block is present, which is optional per the grammar (`[ INDENT , "→" , "FALLBACK" , ... ]` is bracketed, i.e. optional).
- Nothing in the `HITL`/`AUTO` rules requires that the automatically-resolvable case still be reachable only via a human; per the `AUTO` execution table, when `AUTO` is present and the decision is sufficiently determined, "The outcome `<name>.<answer>` is established exactly as if a human had given that answer," after which `WHEN` evaluates normally.

## Snippet 2

```text
DELEGATE implementation AUTO
VERIFY implementation
```

**INVALID.**

- This is the spec's own explicit invalid example: "Invalid, because `AUTO` applies only to `HITL`: `DELEGATE implementation AUTO`" (AUTO section).
- The `AUTO` section also states directly: "`AUTO` is not a statement of its own and applies to no other statement. `AUTO` applies only to `HITL`."
- The grammar confirms this: `simple = ("REQUIRE"|"DELEGATE"|"VERIFY"|"TRANSITION"|"EMIT") , argument , NL` — there is no `AUTO` token permitted in the `simple` production; `AUTO` only appears inside the `hitl` production, after the closing parenthesis of `HITL(...)`.
- Since this violates a rule stated as "invalid" and "applies only to," the whole program is not well-formed and must be rejected as a whole (Programs, fragments, and validity section).

## Snippet 3

```text
AUTO HITL:deploy("Deploy to production?")
WHEN deploy.yes
  → EMIT deployment
```

**INVALID.**

- The `AUTO` section is explicit about placement: "`AUTO` is written directly after the closing parenthesis of `HITL(...)`, on the same line." Here `AUTO` precedes `HITL` instead of following the closing `)`.
- The grammar likewise fixes the position: `hitl = "HITL" , [":" , identifier] , "(" , string , ")" , ["AUTO"] , NL , ...` — `AUTO` must come after `")"`, not before `"HITL"`. `AUTO HITL:deploy(...)` does not match this production (there is no production where `AUTO` precedes the `HITL` keyword).
- The Syntax table also fixes the form as `HITL[:<name>]("<message>") [AUTO]`, i.e. `HITL` first, `AUTO` last.
- Because this line cannot be derived from the grammar, the program is not well-formed and must be rejected as a whole (Programs, fragments, and validity section).

## Snippet 4

```text
AUTO
  WHEN decision.uncertain
    → HITL("Approve?")
```

**INVALID.**

- This is essentially the spec's own explicit invalid example: "Also invalid, because `AUTO` never contains a flow and uncertainty is not a condition: `AUTO` / `WHEN decision.uncertain` / `→ HITL("Approve?")`" (AUTO section).
- Supporting rules from the same section: "`AUTO` is not a control-flow container. It does not contain `WHEN`, `→`, or an arbitrary flow." and "Uncertainty is not a DSL condition, state, variable, or keyword."
- Structurally, `AUTO` is not one of the constructs the Scope rules allow to have indented children ("These lines may have indented children: `LOOP`, `WHEN`, `FORK`, `HITL` (only for `→ FALLBACK`), `→ FALLBACK`, and every `→` item."; `AUTO` is absent from this list), and it has no entry in the grammar as a standalone statement at all — it only appears as an optional trailing token inside `hitl`. An indented block under a bare `AUTO` line is therefore also invalid under Scope rule 6: "Any other indentation is invalid."
- Hence the program is not well-formed and must be rejected as a whole.

## Notes on assumptions

The specification determines all four cases without ambiguity — Snippets 2 and 4 are near-verbatim copies of the spec's own worked "invalid" examples, and Snippets 1 and 3 are resolved directly by the explicit `AUTO` placement rule and the `hitl` grammar production. No assumption beyond the given text was required for these four judgments.
