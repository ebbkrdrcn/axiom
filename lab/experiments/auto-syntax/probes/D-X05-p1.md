# Answers

## Snippet 1

```text
AUTO
HITL:deploy("Deploy to production?")
WHEN deploy.yes
  → EMIT deployment
```

**VALID**

- The grammar for `hitl` is `[ "AUTO", NL ], "HITL", [ ":" , identifier ], "(" , string , ")" , NL , [ ... FALLBACK ... ]`. Here `AUTO` occupies its own line, immediately followed by `HITL:deploy("Deploy to production?")` on the next line, both at the same indentation (column 0) — this matches the Syntax rule for `AUTO`: "`AUTO` is written on its own line directly before a `HITL`, at the same indentation."
- A `FALLBACK` block is optional in the grammar (`[ INDENT , "→" , "FALLBACK" , ... ]`), so its absence does not make the snippet invalid.
- `HITL:deploy(...)` establishes the outcome `deploy.<answer>`, per the `HITL` rule: "The response establishes the outcome `<name>.<answer>`, where `<answer>` is one of the answers tested by the `WHEN <name>.<answer>` constructs that follow." The following `WHEN deploy.yes` is exactly such a construct testing `deploy.yes`, satisfying `condition = identifier , "." , identifier`.
- The whole thing parses as a well-formed `block`: a `hitl` statement followed by a `when` statement, both at the same indentation, per `block = statement , { statement }`.

## Snippet 2

```text
AUTO
DELEGATE implementation
VERIFY implementation
```

**INVALID**

- The specification states directly: "`AUTO` applies only to `HITL`," and gives the explicit invalid example: "Invalid, because `AUTO` applies only to `HITL`: `AUTO` / `DELEGATE implementation`." This snippet is that exact pattern — `AUTO` precedes a `DELEGATE` statement, not a `HITL`.
- The grammar confirms this: `AUTO` only appears inside the `hitl` production (`[ "AUTO" , NL ] , "HITL" , ...`); there is no grammar rule allowing `AUTO` before `simple` (which covers `DELEGATE`). An `AUTO` line not immediately followed by `HITL` is explicitly called invalid: "An `AUTO` line that is not immediately followed by a `HITL` is invalid."

## Snippet 3

```text
AUTO HITL:deploy("Deploy to production?")
WHEN deploy.yes
  → EMIT deployment
```

**INVALID**

- The Syntax rule requires: "`AUTO` is written on its own line directly before a `HITL`, at the same indentation." Here `AUTO` and `HITL:deploy(...)` are written on the same line, not as two separate lines.
- The grammar makes this explicit: `hitl = [ "AUTO" , NL ] , "HITL" , ...` — a `NL` (newline) token is required between `AUTO` and `HITL`. Placing them on one line does not produce that `NL`, so the construct does not match the `hitl` production and the statement is not well-formed.

## Snippet 4

```text
AUTO
  WHEN decision.uncertain
    → HITL("Approve?")
```

**INVALID**

- This matches the specification's own explicit invalid example: "Also invalid, because `AUTO` never contains a flow and uncertainty is not a condition: `AUTO` / `  WHEN decision.uncertain` / `    → HITL("Approve?")`."
- Supporting rules: "`AUTO` is not a control-flow container. It does not contain `WHEN`, `→`, or an arbitrary flow." Here `AUTO` has an indented child (`WHEN ...`), which the grammar does not allow — `AUTO` is only ever the fixed prefix token in `hitl = [ "AUTO" , NL ] , "HITL" , ...` and cannot itself open an `INDENT`/`block`.
- Additionally, "Uncertainty is not a DSL condition, state, variable, or keyword," so `decision.uncertain` is not a legal `condition` for `WHEN` in this DSL, and `AUTO` is also not immediately followed by a `HITL` (it is followed by `WHEN`), independently violating "An `AUTO` line that is not immediately followed by a `HITL` is invalid."

---

**Note on assumptions:** No assumptions beyond the specification's own text were needed — Snippets 2 and 4 correspond directly to examples the specification itself labels "invalid," and Snippets 1 and 3 were checked against the explicit `hitl` grammar production and the "own line, same indentation" rule for `AUTO`.
