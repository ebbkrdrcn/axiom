# DSL Analysis: WHEN Flow

## Program Structure

```
VERIFY review
WHEN review.rejected
  → DELEGATE correction
      VERIFY correction
  → TRANSITION "Rework"
EMIT review-report
```

---

## Answers

### 1. Which statements belong to the WHEN flow?

The following statements belong to the WHEN flow:

1. `DELEGATE correction`
2. `VERIFY correction`
3. `TRANSITION "Rework"`

**Reasoning:** According to the **Indentation and scope** rules (line 508), a line belongs to the nearest preceding line that is indented less than it. The two `→` items and their nested content form the complete WHEN flow.

---

### 2. Sequential or concurrent, and in what order?

**Sequential execution, in this order:**

1. `DELEGATE correction`
2. `VERIFY correction` 
3. `TRANSITION "Rework"`

**Reasoning:** From the **WHEN** section (line 105-136) and Rule 4 of **Execution in ten rules** (line 60): "The `→` items under a `WHEN` or a `FALLBACK` form **one sequential flow**." The specification explicitly states they are sequential, not concurrent (concurrent flow requires `FORK`, which is not used here).

---

### 3. Is `VERIFY correction` part of the WHEN flow?

**Yes.**

`VERIFY correction` is nested under the `→ DELEGATE correction` item (it is indented more deeply). According to the indentation rules and the example at lines 531-542, lines nested under a `→` item are part of that statement's flow. Therefore, `VERIFY correction` is a child of `DELEGATE correction` and belongs to the WHEN flow.

---

### 4. Is `EMIT review-report` part of the WHEN flow? Is it executed?

**Not part of the WHEN flow. Yes, it is executed.**

- `EMIT review-report` is at the base indentation level, not indented under the WHEN block. It is outside the WHEN scope.
- It **is executed** because, according to Rule 2 of **WHEN** (line 117): "Run the flow... Then continue after the `WHEN`." After the WHEN flow completes successfully, execution continues sequentially with the next statement.

---

### 5. If `VERIFY review` had established `review.accepted`, which statements would execute?

Only these statements would execute:

1. `VERIFY review`
2. `EMIT review-report`

**Reasoning:** The WHEN condition is `review.rejected`. If `VERIFY review` established `review.accepted` instead, the condition would be false (from Rule 7: "Before anything establishes it, every `WHEN` on it is false" and the outcome must match exactly). According to Rule 2 (line 118): "The condition is false → Skip the flow and continue after the `WHEN`." The WHEN flow would be skipped entirely, and execution would jump directly to `EMIT review-report`.

---

## Assumptions

None required. All answers are determined by the specification's explicit rules for indentation, sequential flow, and WHEN evaluation.
