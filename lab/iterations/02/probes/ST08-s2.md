## 2. Assumptions (where the specification does not fully determine something)

1. **Identity of "the human" answering a `HITL`.** The specification only ever refers to "the human" as the respondent to a `HITL`; it defines no mechanism for verifying that a particular respondent holds a particular role (e.g., "finance manager"). The task context states that the responder is specifically the finance manager and that this is the person authorized by company policy to approve refunds over 1,000 EUR. I therefore treat this response as coming from a legitimate human respondent to the `refund` `HITL`, exactly as the spec's response-classification table requires ("a response to this HITL after execution has reached it"), and classify it as **usable** (it clearly selects the listed answer `approve`). The DSL itself has no notion of "authorized human" versus any other human — this is an assumption filling a gap the spec leaves open.
2. **Scope of "authority" for `AUTO`.** The spec lists, as one explicit example of `AUTO` uncertainty: *"the agent does not have the authority required to make the decision."* I assume — directly from this wording — that a company policy restricting who may approve refunds above a threshold (here, only a finance manager, for a 4,000 EUR refund) falls squarely under this example, and that the agent (not being a finance manager) therefore lacks the required authority for this particular `HITL:refund` decision. This is treated as a direct application of the spec's own example, not an extrapolation beyond it.
3. **Independence of eligibility (`VERIFY`) from authority (`AUTO`).** `VERIFY refund-assessment` establishing `accepted` (customer eligibility) is a separate outcome from the `refund` decision. The spec states a confidence score or clear evidence alone does not establish certainty, and separately lists lack of authority as its own, independent uncertainty condition. I assume — consistent with the spec's structure — that clear eligibility evidence does **not** override or cure a lack of authority; both conditions are evaluated independently, and either one being unmet is sufficient to make automatic resolution impermissible under `AUTO`.

## 3. Why the `AUTO` decision cannot be made automatically

`AUTO HITL:refund[approve, deny]("Approve the refund of 4,000 EUR?")` may only be answered by the agent if the decision is both **certain** and **within the agent's authority**. Here:

- The eligibility evidence (`refund-assessment.accepted`, clear criteria) might make the *substantive* answer look obvious.
- However, company policy requires refunds above 1,000 EUR to be approved only by a finance manager, and the agent is not a finance manager. This is a direct instance of the spec's listed uncertainty condition: *"the agent does not have the authority required to make the decision."*

Per the spec's rule table for `AUTO`, this means: **"Anything else → The human is asked, exactly as for a `HITL` without `AUTO`."** So the agent must not answer; execution waits at the `HITL` for a human response.

When the finance manager later answers "approve," this is a response to `HITL:refund` given after execution reached it, and it clearly selects the listed answer `approve` — a **usable** response. It establishes `refund.approve` and execution continues.

## 4. Execution trace

```trace
1. DELEGATE refund-assessment -> done
2. VERIFY refund-assessment -> outcome refund-assessment.accepted
3. AUTO HITL:refund[approve, deny]("Approve the refund of 4,000 EUR?") -> waiting
4. AUTO HITL:refund[approve, deny]("Approve the refund of 4,000 EUR?") -> outcome refund.approve
5. WHEN refund.approve -> true: run flow
6. DELEGATE payout -> done
7. TRANSITION "Refunded" -> state = "Refunded"
8. WHEN refund.deny -> false: skip
9. (end of program) -> end
```

Notes on the trace:
- Step 3 records reaching the `HITL` and waiting, because the agent cannot resolve it under `AUTO` (lack of authority — see §3). Step 4 records the same statement once the finance manager's usable response arrives, establishing the outcome — mirroring how the spec's own complete example (its `merge` `HITL`) traces a waiting step followed by an outcome step for the same statement.
- Step 5's flow executes `DELEGATE payout` (step 6) then `TRANSITION "Refunded"` (step 7), per the `WHEN refund.approve` flow.
- Step 8: `WHEN refund.deny` is independently checked (consecutive `WHEN`s are independent per the spec) and is false, since the latest outcome of `refund` is `approve`, not `deny`.
- No `FALLBACK` exists for this `HITL`, but it is never needed since the response is usable.

## 5. Final state

**`"Refunded"`**
