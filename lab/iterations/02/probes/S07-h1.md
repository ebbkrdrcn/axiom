# Answers to DSL Questions

## 1. What does `WAIT` wait for here?

According to the specification (Control / WAIT section):

> The DSL does not name the event. The surrounding process and the runtime determine it.

The specification is explicit that `WAIT` does not declare what it waits for. The runtime and the surrounding context determine the event. In this program, the context suggests the `WAIT` may be waiting for the actor to deliver a result from the `data-migration` delegation, but the DSL itself does not specify this. Only the runtime can determine what event will unblock the `WAIT`.

## 2. What executes immediately after `WAIT` ends?

Immediately after `WAIT` ends, the next statement executes:

```
VERIFY data-migration
```

According to the specification: "After the event, the next statement runs."

## 3. Does reaching `VERIFY data-migration` imply that the migration succeeded?

No. According to the specification (Agentic Operations / VERIFY section):

> The completion of delegated work is not a successful verification.

Reaching the `VERIFY` statement does not imply success. The `VERIFY` statement will evaluate the result and establish one of two outcomes: `data-migration.accepted` or `data-migration.rejected`. But reaching `VERIFY` itself tells us nothing about whether the migration succeeded—only that it is being evaluated now.

## 4. What happens if the awaited event never occurs?

According to the specification (Control / WAIT section):

> There is no timeout. If the event never comes, execution stays at the `WAIT`.

Execution will remain suspended at the `WAIT` statement indefinitely. No further statements will execute unless and until an external event arrives at the runtime.
