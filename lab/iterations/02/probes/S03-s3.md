```text
FORK
  → DELEGATE security-review
      VERIFY security-review
  → DELEGATE performance-review
      VERIFY performance-review
JOIN
TRANSITION "Release"
```

## Assumptions

1. **Identifiers for the two review work items.** The requirement names "a security review" and "a performance review" but the spec does not dictate identifier spelling. I used `security-review` and `performance-review` (valid identifiers: letters, digits, `-`, `_`, starting with a letter).

2. **No branching on the verification outcome.** The requirement only says "each review is delegated and then verified" and to "continue only after both flows have finished." It does not say what should happen if a review is `rejected` vs `accepted`. Per the spec, `VERIFY` does not branch by itself, and only a `WHEN` would make the flow depend on the outcome. Since the requirement gives no criteria for differing behavior on accept/reject, I assumed no `WHEN` is needed and the process proceeds to `JOIN` and then `TRANSITION "Release"` regardless of either outcome.

3. **"Continue only after both flows have finished" maps directly to `JOIN`.** Per the spec, `JOIN` waits for every branch of the nearest `FORK` to complete, and a branch is complete once its last statement (here, `VERIFY`) is done — including if that `VERIFY` establishes `rejected`. This matches "each review is delegated and then verified within its own parallel flow" combined with "continue only after both flows have finished."

4. **No `HITL`, `REQUIRE`, `WAIT`, `LOOP`, `STOP`, or `EMIT` are needed.** The requirement does not mention any human decision, a precondition to wait on, repetition, early termination, or an artifact to produce, so none of these constructs were introduced.

5. **`TRANSITION "Release"` is unconditional and final.** The requirement says to "then transition to state 'Release'" with no further steps described, so this is the last statement of the program, and execution ends normally afterward (per rule 10 of "Execution in ten rules").

6. **Two branches satisfy `FORK`'s minimum-branch rule (V9).** Exactly two concurrent branches (security review, performance review) are declared under `FORK`, satisfying "fewer than two branches" being invalid.

7. **Each branch's internal sequencing.** "Each review is delegated and then verified within its own parallel flow" was interpreted as: within a single `FORK` branch, `DELEGATE <review>` runs first, then `VERIFY <review>` runs on the same subject name, sequentially, per the branch's own thread of control — consistent with rule 4 ("The `→` items under a `FORK` are separate concurrent branches") and the nesting rule for children of a `→` item.
