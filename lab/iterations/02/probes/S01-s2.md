```text
DELEGATE implementation
VERIFY implementation
AUTO HITL:merge[approved, declined]("Merge this change?")
WHEN merge.approved
  → TRANSITION "Merged"
WHEN merge.declined
  → TRANSITION "Rework"
```

## Assumptions

1. **Answer names for the merge decision.** The requirement says "the decision is to merge" / "the decision is not to merge" but does not give literal answer tokens. I used `approved` and `declined` (the same pair used in the specification's own examples for a merge-type `HITL`) as the two listed answers for `HITL:merge[...]`.

2. **No `FALLBACK` for the merge `HITL`.** The requirement describes only two outcomes (merge / not merge) and says nothing about what happens if the human gives no answer or an unusable one. Since a `FALLBACK` is optional in the grammar (`[fallback]`), I omitted it. Per the spec, this means an insufficient or unavailable response simply ends execution (as with `STOP`), with no outcome established and no `TRANSITION`.

3. **Outcome of `VERIFY implementation` is not branched on.** The requirement says "verify it" but does not say the process should behave differently based on `accepted` vs `rejected` — only the merge decision determines the two transitions. So `VERIFY implementation` is included (establishing `implementation.accepted`/`implementation.rejected`) but no `WHEN` tests it, since the requirement gives no such branching logic. This is a valid, if perhaps incomplete-looking, use of `VERIFY` per the spec ("`VERIFY` does not branch... only a `WHEN` makes the flow depend on it"); a `WHEN` on it was not required by the requirement.

4. **Single delegation, not a loop.** The requirement describes a one-shot "delegate then verify then decide," not a retry loop, so no `LOOP` is used.

5. **`AUTO` applies to the merge `HITL`.** "the agent may make the merge decision itself when it is clearly determined by the available information and criteria; otherwise a human must be asked" maps directly to the spec's `AUTO HITL` semantics (agent decides only when certain and within its authority; otherwise the human is asked), so this was modeled as `AUTO HITL:merge[...]` rather than a plain `HITL`.

6. **Identifier `merge` as the `HITL` name.** The requirement does not name the decision; `merge` was chosen as a descriptive identifier consistent with the specification's own merge-decision example.

7. **No `REQUIRE`, `FORK`, or other constructs.** The requirement does not mention any precondition, concurrency, or additional human requests, so only `DELEGATE`, `VERIFY`, `AUTO HITL`, `WHEN`, and `TRANSITION` were used.
