# Answer

## 1. Which of the two decisions may the agent make by itself, and under what condition?

Only the **wording** decision (`HITL:wording("Short or detailed release notes?") AUTO`) may be resolved by the agent itself. The **release** decision (`HITL:release("Approve the release?")`) has no `AUTO` marker, and the spec states plainly: *"`HITL` must not be silently replaced by an agent decision unless that `HITL` is explicitly marked with `AUTO`."* Since `HITL:release` is not marked `AUTO`, the agent may never answer it itself, under any condition — a human must always be asked.

For `wording`, the condition under which the agent may resolve it automatically is spelled out in the `AUTO` section: automatic resolution is permitted **only when the agent can determine a single protocol-compatible decision from the available information and applicable criteria**, i.e. when the decision is "sufficiently determined." Concretely, per the spec's execution table for `AUTO`, this requires that none of the listed sources of uncertainty apply — in particular:

- the required information is not missing or contradictory;
- the applicable criteria (for choosing short vs. detailed notes) are not missing, ambiguous, or contradictory;
- no multiple materially different answers (`short` vs. `detailed`) remain reasonably compatible with the evidence and criteria;
- the result does not depend on an unresolved interpretation;
- the agent has the authority to make this decision.

If all of that holds, the agent resolves `wording` itself, establishing `wording.short` or `wording.detailed` "exactly as if a human had given that answer," and the human is not asked. If any of those conditions fails (uncertainty exists), the human is asked instead, exactly as for a `HITL` without `AUTO`.

## 2. All automated checks are green and the agent is highly confident the release should be approved. May it answer "Approve the release?" itself?

No. `HITL:release("Approve the release?")` carries no `AUTO` marker in the program as written, and per the spec `AUTO` is written directly after the closing parenthesis of a specific `HITL(...)` and "applies only to `HITL`" — as the one statement it is attached to. It is not a global or inherited setting, so the fact that the *other* `HITL` (`wording`) has `AUTO` has no bearing on `release`. Since `HITL` "must not be silently replaced by an agent decision unless that `HITL` is explicitly marked with `AUTO`," and this one is not, the agent must not decide it, regardless of confidence.

This holds independent of how confident the agent is: even where `AUTO` *does* apply, the spec explicitly states "a confidence score alone does not establish certainty." So even under the (counterfactual) assumption that `AUTO` were present on `release`, "highly confident" would not by itself be sufficient to justify automatic resolution — but here the point is moot, because `AUTO` is simply absent from this `HITL`. The human must be asked "Approve the release?", and execution waits at that `HITL` for a usable human response (or its `FALLBACK`, if one existed — none is defined here).

## 3. If the wording decision is not clearly determined, what happens?

Per the `AUTO` execution table: when `AUTO` is present but the decision is uncertain (or outside the agent's authority), "the human is asked, as for a `HITL` without `AUTO`." So the agent does **not** guess or pick an answer — it falls back to the normal `HITL` behavior: the flow suspends at `HITL:wording(...)` until a usable human response naming `short` or `detailed` becomes available (this is not a "fallback" in the `FALLBACK`-mechanism sense; the spec is explicit that "`AUTO` and `FALLBACK` are different mechanisms" and `FALLBACK` applies only afterwards, if the human's response itself is unavailable or insufficient).

Once a usable human response is obtained, the outcome `wording.short` or `wording.detailed` is established from that response, and execution continues with the statement after the `HITL:wording` construct — i.e. the two `WHEN` constructs are evaluated in order, and whichever one matches the established outcome runs its `→ DELEGATE ...` flow. Execution then proceeds to `HITL:release`.

**Note on what the specification does not determine here (assumption stated explicitly):** the given program attaches no `FALLBACK` flow to `HITL:wording`. The spec defines `FALLBACK` as being used "only when the expected response cannot be used to continue the normal flow" (response unavailable or insufficient), and says the DSL "has no timeout syntax; the runtime decides when a missing response counts as unavailable." Since no `FALLBACK` is written under this `HITL`, the specification does not say what happens if the human's response turns out to be unavailable or insufficient — there is no fallback flow to fall through to. Under the general `WAIT`/`HITL` principle that the flow "waits ... until either a usable response is available or its `FALLBACK` flow is used," and given no `FALLBACK` exists, the only spec-consistent behavior is that execution simply continues to wait (indefinitely, if necessary) until a usable response is actually given — analogous to `WAIT`'s rule that "if the event never becomes available, execution remains suspended." This is my assumption to fill the gap the specification leaves open for the no-`FALLBACK` case; the spec never states an alternative such as auto-terminating or defaulting to one branch, and both of those would contradict "the agent must not assume, fabricate, or substitute" a decision.
